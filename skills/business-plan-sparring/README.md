# Business plan sparring partner

> A listen-first sparring partner that sets the foundations of a founder's business plan (the purpose, the stage, the few drivers that matter, how cash really moves) and ends with a one-page plan brief, before anyone builds a model.

Part of the [Finance Cookbook](../../README.md), first step of the [founder's kit](../../paths/founder.md). This is a **skill**, not a black box: [`SKILL.md`](./SKILL.md) is plain, readable Markdown. Open it and read it before you install it, that is the point.

## The problem

A founder sits down to build a business plan and one of two things happens. Either they freeze in front of an empty spreadsheet, or they hand their notes to an agent, which builds a confident five-year model with forty lines, insurance splits and an EBITDA to 2029, without asking a single question. The founder cannot explain it, so they do not use it.

The hard part is never the arithmetic. It is deciding what the plan is for, how deep it should go at this stage, and which three to five assumptions actually move the outcome. That is a conversation, and it has to happen before the model.

## What it does

- **Starts with the stage and the purpose**, as quick multiple-choice questions: idea, first customers, revenue or raise; a go / no-go, a meeting, a raise or a steering plan. The stage sets the depth, so an idea gets a 12-month view, not a five-year one.
- **Asks short Socratic questions**, three at most per turn, with choices when it can. It asks the question that makes the founder see the weak assumption instead of handing over the answer.
- **Looks for the drivers that are not financial lines**: leads, conversion, capacity, churn, sales cycle. A plan built on those is defensible; "revenue grows 20% a year" is not.
- **Separates revenue from cash** where they drift: billed upfront and earned over time (deferred revenue), paid late, stock bought before selling, grants and loans.
- **Keeps the first pass stupid simple**, then challenges the two or three load-bearing assumptions like a seed investor or a banker would.
- **Ends with a plan brief**: one page with the purpose, stage, drivers, costs, cash timing, funding, KPIs and open questions. The founder owns it, a coach can review it, and the [plan-to-model](../plan-to-model/) skill builds from it.
- **Works with a coach in the room.** When a mentor or programme coach runs the session with a founder, it addresses both.

## Make it yours

The file is meant to be forked. A coach can translate it, change the questions to their own method, add the cash patterns their founders keep missing, or tighten the brief to the format their programme asks for. Keep the hard rules: they are what stops the agent from building big before anyone has thought.

## Where it breaks

A conversation can set the foundations. It cannot hold a model: change one assumption and the table is re-derived by hand, numbers quietly get hardcoded, nothing persists between sessions, and nobody can say where a figure came from. The moment the plan has to survive a second meeting, a bank or a raise, the brief needs a real model behind it.

When you hit that wall, run [plan-to-model](../plan-to-model/) on the brief, reach for [`FINANCE.md`](https://github.com/layerzlabs/finance-md) to pin the conventions and the context, and [Layerz](https://layerz.cc) to give the model a structured, versioned home where scenarios are branches you can compare.
